The key rules for flying in the UK Open category. This is a summary. The CAA’s Drone and Model Aircraft Code is the official version.

Everyone, every flight

  • Stay below 120 m (400 ft) from the closest point of the ground.
  • Keep it in sight. Always see your drone with your own eyes. If you use FPV goggles, you need an observer next to you.
  • Never fly over crowds or large groups of people.
  • Check the airspace before you fly. Don’t enter an airport or airfield Flight Restriction Zone without permission.
  • Keep away from emergency services activity such as accidents, fires and police incidents.
  • Carry your Flyer ID, and put your Operator ID on the drone where one is needed.
  • Turn on Remote ID if your drone needs it (see below).
  • Respect privacy. Think before you film people, and follow data protection law if you record them.
  • You are responsible for every flight. Check the drone, the battery and the weather first.

What applies to your drone

Choose the class mark on your drone, or its weight if it has no class mark.

Good to know

  • Flyer ID is free. It comes from the CAA’s online test and lasts 5 years.
  • Operator ID is for the person or organisation responsible for the drone. You must be 18 or over, it costs an annual fee and you renew it every year.
  • EU C‑class drones (C0 to C4) can be flown as the matching UK class until 31 December 2027.
  • The A2 Certificate of Competency (A2 CofC) comes from a CAA‑recognised training provider. It lets you fly a UK2 drone closer to people.
  • Outside these limits? Flying closer to people, beyond sight or above 120 m is the Specific category. It needs an Operational Authorisation from the CAA.
